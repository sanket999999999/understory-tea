Kabuse · the covering

Twenty days in the dark.

In late April, growers draw straw and black cloth over the bushes. The plants reach for sun that never comes, and the young leaves turn soft, thin and deep green.

What the shade keeps

Less bitterness. More sweetness.

Without sunlight, the leaf stops turning its L-theanine into bitter catechins. That amino acid stays in the cup as a savory, brothy sweetness the Japanese call umami.

Brewing gyokuro

Cool water. Small cup. Patience.

Boiling water pulls out the bitterness the shade worked so hard to hold back. Treat it gently.

You can steep the same leaves three times. Raise the water about 10 °C and shorten the time for each round.

  1. 5 gleaf

    Measure the leaf

    About two teaspoons into a small kyusu pot, for two small cups.

  2. 60 °Cwater

    Cool the water

    Pour boiled water into the cups, then into a pitcher. Each pour drops it roughly 10 °C.

  3. 60 mlvolume

    Pour just enough

    Gyokuro is served in sips. Barely cover the leaves.

  4. 90 ssteep

    Wait, then pour to the last drop

    Alternate between cups so each gets the same strength. The final drops are the richest.
